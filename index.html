Stay ahead of the maintenance on everything you own.

ReliaLife is a maintenance tracker for your house, your cars, and your equipment, and for the machines a small business runs on. Tell it what you have, and it tells you what needs doing and when. It runs on iPhone and in any web browser, on the same account.

What ReliaLife Does

One app that keeps track of the maintenance on everything you own, and tells you when something needs attention.

You add your assets: the house, the truck, the espresso machine. Big things break into components, so the furnace filter and the water heater each get their own record. Then each one gets its maintenance tasks. A task can repeat on a time interval, a usage interval like miles or running hours, or both at once, the way an oil change is due at 5,000 miles or six months, whichever comes first.

The dashboard sorts it all into three piles: overdue, due soon, and further out. When you do the work, you mark it done, add a note if you want, and the app builds a service history you can actually look things up in later. Notifications keep the quiet stuff from sneaking past you.

You don't have to invent the schedules yourself. For many cars, ReliaLife carries the maintenance schedule from the manufacturer's own guide for your exact year and model. The same goes for a growing list of commercial machines, espresso machines and brewery gear among them. Where a manufacturer publishes nothing, the app falls back on sensible general practice and says so, instead of dressing a guess up as fact.

One app, two modes

ReliaBase is the personal side and what you start in: your household, laid out simply. ReliaPro is the business side: the same tracking, plus the structure a shop needs, like locations, equipment tags, staff roles, and daily checklists.

Switching is one setting. Open Settings, pick the mode, and the app changes over. Nothing is installed, nothing is lost, and you can flip back whenever you like. Plenty of people use both: the house in ReliaBase, the shop in ReliaPro, one account.

Who it's for

ReliaLife started as a personal tool, and that's still its heart: anyone who wants their stuff to last and is tired of asking "when did I last change that filter?" But the same habits matter even more to a small business whose machines are the business. When the washer at a laundromat or the group head at a coffee bar goes down, the day's revenue goes with it, and most small shops don't have a maintenance planner on staff. ReliaPro is that planner.

Getting Started

Create an account with an email and password, either in the iPhone app or at the web app. Then add your first asset: pick what kind of thing it is, give it a name, and fill in the basics. For a car that's year, make, and model, and it's worth being exact, because that's how the app finds the manufacturer's schedule.

After the asset, the app offers to break it into components and to set up tasks. The suggested tasks are a starting list, not homework: tick the ones that apply, skip the rest. When you accept a task you'll be asked when it was last done. Answer as best you remember, because that's what makes the due dates honest from day one instead of everything showing up as overdue.

The Dashboard

The dashboard is the home screen and the only screen you need on a normal day. It sorts every task you have into overdue, due soon, and coming up. Tap anything to see the details or mark it done. The quick links at the bottom take you to your assets, the full task list, People, and Settings.

Assets and Components

An asset is a thing you own. A component is a part of it that gets serviced on its own: the house is an asset, the HVAC filter and the water heater are components. Splitting things this way keeps each task attached to the part it belongs to, so history stays useful.

When something leaves your life, you have two options. Deactivate retires the asset but keeps its history, which is the right call for a car you sold. Delete is permanent. Deactivated assets can be reviewed and restored from the web app.

Tasks and Intervals

A task is a piece of maintenance that repeats. It can repeat by time (every 90 days), by usage (every 5,000 miles or every 200 running hours), or by both at once, in which case it comes due at whichever lands first, the way an oil change works.

For usage-based tasks, keep the reading current: update the odometer or hour meter now and then, and the due dates follow along. You can add your own custom tasks anywhere, and edit any interval, including the suggested ones. If a schedule feels too tight for how you actually use the thing, change it. It's your stuff.

Marking Work Done

When work happens, mark the task done. You can set the date it was actually done, if that wasn't today, and add a note: what it cost, what parts went in, what looked worn. Each completion lands in History, per asset and across everything. Six months later, when the same noise comes back, the note is where you left it.

Notifications

Turn notifications on in Settings and pick how often you want to hear from the app. Reminders arrive grouped into summaries rather than one ping per task, so a busy week doesn't turn into a wall of noise. If you'd rather just check the dashboard on your own schedule, leave them off; nothing else depends on them.

Sharing with People

The People screen lets more than one person work the same lists. Send an invite and the other person joins with a code. Invites expire, so a code that's floating around loose goes stale on its own.

For households, the link runs both ways: you're a member of their account, they're a member of yours, and each of you keeps your own records. For a business, it runs one way: staff join the shop's account, because the shop's equipment is the shop's. Each member gets a role that says what they can do, from full admin down to view-only.

Phone and Web

The same account works in the iPhone app and at the web app, and they share their data through the cloud. On the phone, changes save to the device first, so the app works fine with no signal, then sync up. On the web, you're working with the cloud copy directly.

In practice the phone is the tool you carry and the web is where the bigger setup jobs are comfortable: building out a business's equipment, reviewing plans on a real keyboard, and managing deactivated assets, which lives on the web only.

Switching Modes

Open Settings and pick ReliaBase or ReliaPro. That's the whole procedure. The switch changes what the app shows, not what it stores, so you can move between your household and your business freely, and nothing is lost when you do.

ReliaPro: First Setup

Buildout is most comfortable on the web, and reading and doing work fine on the phone. Start with the structure: create your business, then its location or locations. The type of site you pick shapes which systems the app suggests, so a brewery gets brewing suggestions and not laundry ones. Add the systems that match how you think about the place: brewing, cold side, front of house.

Then register the equipment. Pick each machine's type and its make and model from the equipment list, say how critical it is to you (high, medium, or low), and the app suggests a short tag, like MAIN-BREW-PUMP-01, so every machine has a name people can say out loud. If a machine isn't in the list, add it by hand; nothing requires the catalog.

ReliaPro: The PM Plan

With assets registered, generate the maintenance plan. Where we have the manufacturer's service schedule for your make and model, tasks come from it with the source document cited, and those intervals are never stretched or shrunk. Where there's no published schedule, tasks come from general practice for that kind of machine, and they're labeled that way so you always know which is which.

Criticality decides how much of the plan you get: a high-critical machine gets the full treatment, a low-critical one gets the essentials. It never drops anything safety-related. The generated plan is a proposal, so review it and cut what your shop genuinely doesn't do. The Preventative Maintenance module then shows the work grouped by site and system, most critical first.

ReliaPro: Rounds

Anything due weekly or faster isn't really scheduled maintenance; it's a walk. Rounds collects those quick checks into checklists so they don't clutter the PM schedule. Build a site's rounds from a pick list where everything starts ticked, and untick what your shop doesn't do. A check you leave out isn't remembered against you; it's simply offered again next time you build.

Running a round is tapping through it: each check gets an OK, a flag with a note for anything that looks wrong, or a skip. Skipping asks whether to drop the check for this round or keep it open to finish later, and a walk in progress can be put down and picked back up on the same device. One activity that spans several machines, like washing four blind filters, shows up once with a checkbox per machine. Every completed walk is saved with who ran it and when, flags first when you read it back.

ReliaPro: Staff and Roles

Invite staff from the People screen with the role that fits: admin runs the account, editor can change the plan, doer can execute work and run rounds, and viewer can look. Whether someone can invite others is a separate switch from what they can touch. When someone leaves, revoke their membership and the records stay put, because they were always the shop's.
